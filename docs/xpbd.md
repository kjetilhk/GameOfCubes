# Extended Position-Based Dynamics (XPBD) Engine

The core physics engine is implemented in [`src/xpbd.js`](file:///c:/Projects/GameOfCubes/src/xpbd.js). It is completely standalone, with zero DOM or rendering dependencies, exporting `XPBD` (and alias `CubePhysics`) for both browsers and CommonJS environments.

The formulation is based on Müller et al., *"Detailed Rigid Body Simulation with Extended Position Based Dynamics"* (2020), extended with non-inertial reference frame dynamics, edge distance constraints, hydrostatic volume preservation, and spatial hashing.

---

## 1. XPBD Mathematical Formulation

Standard Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) enforces constraints by directly projecting positions, but constraint stiffness depends artificially on the time step $h$ and iteration count. XPBD resolves this by introducing physical compliance $\alpha$ (the inverse of physical stiffness, $\alpha = 1 / k$), allowing constraints to behave like physical elastic springs independent of time step size.

### 1.1 The Compliance Equation

For a constraint $C(\mathbf{x}) = 0$, the Lagrange multiplier increment $\Delta \lambda$ at each substep $h$ is:

$$\Delta \lambda = \frac{-C(\mathbf{x}) - \tilde{\alpha} \lambda}{\sum_i w_i |\nabla C_i|^2 + \tilde{\alpha}}$$

where:
- $\tilde{\alpha} = \frac{\alpha}{h^2}$ is the time-step regularized compliance ($h = \Delta t$).
- $w_i = 1 / m_i$ is the inverse mass of particle $i$.
- $\nabla C_i$ is the constraint gradient with respect to particle position $\mathbf{x}_i$.
- $\lambda$ is the accumulated Lagrange multiplier for the current time step.

Position updates are applied along the constraint gradient:

$$\Delta \mathbf{x}_i = w_i \nabla C_i \Delta \lambda$$

When $\alpha = 0$, $\tilde{\alpha} = 0$, recovering completely rigid / inextensible constraints.

## 2. Simulation Step Pipeline (`World.step()`)

Each frame of fixed duration `dt` (typically $1/60\,\text{s}$) is subdivided into $N$ substeps ($h = \text{dt} / N$, typically $N = 10 \text{ to } 18$):

```
       ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │                  Substep Loop (h = dt/N)               │
       └───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                   │
                     1. Non-Inertial Integration
              (Gravity, Coriolis, Centrifugal, Euler forces)
                                   │
                     2. Solve Particle Constraints
            (Edge distance constraints & Tetrahedral volume)
                                   │
                   3. Particle Collision Resolution
                (Box walls, rigid cubes, self-collision)
                                   │
                     4. Particle Velocity Update
                      & Internal Constraint Damping
                                   │
                   5. Rigid Body Contact Generation
                 (Box-box SAT manifold clipping & walls)
                                   │
                     6. Position Solve (XPBD)
             (Normal depenetration sweeps & Static friction)
                                   │
                   7. Derive Rigid Body Velocities
                     (v = (x - x_prev)/h, w from dq)
                                   │
                     8. Velocity Solve (XPBD)
               (Dynamic friction clamping & Restitution)
                                   │
                     9. Rotational & Linear Damping
                                   │
                                   ▼
```

## 3. Non-Inertial Reference Frame Dynamics

When running on a mobile device or tilting on desktop, the coordinate frame moves and rotates with the phone box. XPBD computes fictitious inertial forces directly in local box space:

$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{a}_{\text{apparent}} - \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}) - 2(\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{v}) - \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} \times \mathbf{r}$$

1. **Apparent Acceleration ($\mathbf{a}_{\text{apparent}}$)**: Combined gravity and linear phone shaking sensed by the accelerometer.
2. **Centrifugal Force ($- \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r})$)**: Outward radial force generated by device rotation rate $\boldsymbol{\omega}$.
3. **Coriolis Force ($- 2(\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{v})$)**: Velocity-dependent lateral deflection felt during rapid twisting.
4. **Euler Force ($- \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} \times \mathbf{r}$)**: Angular acceleration force caused by sudden changes in twist speed ($\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}$).

## 4. Constraint Types

### 4.1 Edge Distance Constraint (Stretch & Shear)
Constrains the Euclidean distance between two particles $\mathbf{x}_1$ and $\mathbf{x}_2$ to their initial rest length $L_0$:

$$C(\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2) = |\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2| - L_0$$

- Gradient: $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_1} C = \mathbf{n}$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_2} C = -\mathbf{n}$, where $\mathbf{n} = \frac{\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2}{|\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2|}$.
- Stretch compliance: $\alpha \approx 10^{-6} \text{ to } 10^{-5}$ (virtually inextensible fabric).
- Shear compliance: $\alpha \approx 0.02$ (flexible diagonal compliance for natural catenary draping).

### 4.2 Tetrahedral Volume Preservation (Incompressibility)
Conserves the hydrostatic volume of each 4-node tetrahedron ($\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \mathbf{x}_3, \mathbf{x}_4$):

$$C(\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \mathbf{x}_3, \mathbf{x}_4) = V - V_0$$

where the signed volume is:

$$V = \frac{1}{6} \left( (\mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_1) \times (\mathbf{x}_3 - \mathbf{x}_1) \right) \cdot (\mathbf{x}_4 - \mathbf{x}_1)$$

The gradient vectors with respect to each vertex represent the outward normals of the opposite triangular faces scaled by face area:
- $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_1} C = \frac{1}{6} (\mathbf{x}_4 - \mathbf{x}_2) \times (\mathbf{x}_3 - \mathbf{x}_2)$
- $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_2} C = \frac{1}{6} (\mathbf{x}_3 - \mathbf{x}_1) \times (\mathbf{x}_4 - \mathbf{x}_1)$
- $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_3} C = \frac{1}{6} (\mathbf{x}_4 - \mathbf{x}_1) \times (\mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_1)$
- $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_4} C = \frac{1}{6} (\mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_1) \times (\mathbf{x}_3 - \mathbf{x}_1)$

With volume compliance set to $\alpha = 0.0$, the soft body achieves **true hydrostatic incompressibility** ($< 0.0001\%$ volume loss even under extreme deformation).

## 5. Collision Detection & Manifold Generation

### 5.1 Cube–Cube SAT (Separating Axis Theorem)
For rigid bodies, collision testing evaluates the 15 potential separating axes:
- 3 face normals of box A
- 3 face normals of box B
- 9 cross products of edge directions from box A and box B

To prevent contact normal popping and jitter, edge axes are only selected if their separation depth is strictly smaller than face axes by a stabilizing margin.

### 5.2 Contact Manifold Clipping
When a face axis is the minimal separating axis, the incident face polygon (4 vertices) is clipped against the reference face's 4 side planes using the Sutherland–Hodgman clipping algorithm. This generates up to 8 contact points per pair, distributing normal contact forces and preventing resting spin.

### 5.3 Spatial Hashing (Cloth Self-Collision)
Self-collision among cloth vertices uses a zero-allocation 3D spatial hash:
- Hash cell size equals vertex collision diameter $2r$.
- Hash index: $H(x, y, z) = (73856093 \cdot x \oplus 19349663 \cdot y \oplus 83492791 \cdot z) \pmod{\text{TABLE\_SIZE}}$.
- Neighboring vertices within $2r$ undergo mutual XPBD non-penetration position projection.

## 6. Solver Iterations & Performance Invariants

- **Alternating Sweep Order**: Contact constraints are solved in alternating order each substep to eliminate bias and directional resting creep.
- **Push Velocity Clamping**: Maximum positional depenetration rate is clamped to `maxPushSpeed = 6.0 m/s` to prevent explosive popping when objects are spawned overlapping.
- **Dynamic Friction Limit**: Dynamic friction impulse is clamped according to Coulomb's law:
  $$\Delta P_t \le \mu_k \frac{\lambda_n}{h}$$
- **Zero Allocation**: Module-level scratch vectors (`_v1`, `_v2`, `_q1`, `_mat1`) and pre-allocated contact pools ensure zero garbage collection overhead during hot simulation steps.
